\section{Introduction}

A foundational question in machine learning is whether autoregressive neural networks can internalize and execute exact sequential algorithms invariant to sequence length. The prevailing paradigm in contemporary language modeling scales autoregressive Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention,brown2020language} to tens or hundreds of billions of parameters over subword Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) vocabularies. While these models exhibit remarkable fluency on conversational tasks, small Transformers trained from scratch notoriously fail on basic algorithmic tasks like multi-digit addition when tested beyond the sequence lengths seen during training \cite{nogueira2021investigating,shen2023positional,jelassi2023length,dziri2023faith,mcleish2024transformers,kazemnejad2023impact}. Frontier models with chain-of-thought or tool access do considerably better; our statements concern small models trained from scratch. While such a Transformer can memorize or interpolate addition patterns up to its training ceiling $N_{\text{train}}$, its accuracy drops precipitously beyond that boundary (falling to 4.6\% on 14--16 digits and collapsing to 0.0\% on 18- to 20-digit sums in our experiments).

\subsection{Algorithmic Arithmetic and the Success of 2D Embeddings}
\label{sec:algorithmic_math}

Multi-digit integer addition serves as the canonical testbed for algorithmic length generalization: it possesses an unbounded length axis and is governed by a compact, deterministic finite-state machine (FSM) operating right-to-left from the units column ($i = 0$) to the most significant column ($i = N - 1$):
\begin{equation}
s_i = (a_i + b_i + c_{i-1}) \bmod 10, \quad c_i = \left\lfloor \frac{a_i + b_i + c_{i-1}}{10} \right\rfloor
\label{eq:carry_fsm}
\end{equation}
where $a_i, b_i \in \{0, \dots, 9\}$ are the single-digit operands at column $i$, $c_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the binary carry bit emitted to column $i+1$, and $c_{-1} = 0$. Because the entire transition table consists of only $10 \times 10 \times 2 = 200$ discrete rules, executing addition requires $\mathcal{O}(N)$ identical state transitions regardless of whether $N=4$, $N=15$, or $N=50$.

When we trained a standard 18.91M Transformer ($d_{\text{model}}=512, L=6, n_{\text{head}}=8$) with standard one-dimensional rotary embeddings (1D-RoPE \cite{su2024roformer,kazemnejad2023impact}) on 1- to 12-digit additions with explicit column scratchpads \cite{nye2021show,wei2022chain}, it failed completely beyond its training horizon: achieving 98.4\% on seen 1--12 digits and 4.6\% on moderate 14--16 digits, before falling to \textbf{0.0\% accuracy on unseen 18- to 20-digit sums} (Table~\ref{tab:arch_comparison}). Diagnosing these runs revealed that 1D positional encodings assign a flat, monotonically increasing index $t = 0, \dots, T-1$. At unseen sequence lengths ($T \approx 715$ characters for 50 digits, scaling at $\approx 14.3$ tokens per digit), relative distances $(m - n)$ between an answer column and its operand digits take values never encountered during training, inducing positional phase drift and repetitive zero loops where the model repeats blocks like \texttt{(0+0+0)=00} indefinitely:
\begin{equation}
P\big(\texttt{"(0+0+0)=00"} \;\big|\; \texttt{"(0+0+0)=00"}^{\times m}\big) \approx 1
\end{equation}
When alternative 1D architectures were evaluated under identical budgets (Looped Transformers \cite{dehghani2018universal,giannou2023looped}, Hierarchical Reasoning Models, Gated Linear Recurrent Units, Harmonic Oscillators, and discrete Abacus place embeddings \cite{mcleish2024transformers}), all nine baselines fell to 0.0\% on 18--20 digits (Table~\ref{tab:arch_comparison}).

To overcome this failure, we structured the problem onto a \textbf{two-dimensional (2D) coordinate grid} $(p_t, \tau_t) \in \mathbb{N}^2$, where macro row $p$ tracks decimal place-value rank ($10^p$) and micro column $\tau \in [0, 9]$ tracks the intra-column execution step (Table~\ref{tab:2d_matrix_example}A). Using \textbf{Decoupled 2D Rotary Position Embeddings (2D-RoPE)}, matching digits across operands, scratchpad, and answer share identical macro coordinates ($\Delta p = 0$). We found that \textbf{2D embeddings perform exceptionally well on arithmetic}: by preserving the decimal place-value structure of digits invariant to problem length, 2D coordinates eliminate 1D phase drift and enable reliable length extrapolation far beyond the training boundary (e.g., our 1.56M HRM-RoPE baseline extrapolates to 20 digits using Decoupled 2D-RoPE alone; Table~\ref{tab:itemized_math}).

\subsection{From 2D Digits to 2D Characters: The Core Hypothesis}
\label{sec:char_hypothesis}

Standard language models rely on subword BPE tokenization, which clumps arbitrary character and digit substrings into irregular multi-character tokens:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{"12345678"} &\xrightarrow{\text{BPE}} \big[\texttt{"12"}, \texttt{"3"}, \texttt{"45"}, \texttt{"67"}, \texttt{"8"}\big] \\
\texttt{"87654321"} &\xrightarrow{\text{BPE}} \big[\texttt{"8"}, \texttt{"7"}, \texttt{"65"}, \texttt{"43"}, \texttt{"21"}\big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
A single token like \texttt{"45"} fuses the $10^3$ place (\texttt{4}) and the $10^2$ place (\texttt{5}) into one vector at one 1D index, breaking 1-to-1 place-value alignment between operands (Table~\ref{tab:tokenizer_dichotomy}).

While character-level Transformers avoid these subword merging artifacts and preserve character-level boundaries, they still fail to extrapolate multi-digit addition under standard 1D positional encodings, succumbing to identical phase drift. Having established that Decoupled 2D-RoPE fixes addition collapse by maintaining invariant decimal place-value coordinates, we formulated our central hypothesis: \emph{Supplying place-value coordinates through Decoupled 2D-RoPE directly to atomic characters removes this extrapolation collapse in character-level models, and this exact coordinate scheme can be extended to multi-turn tool-calling dialogues without degrading them.}

To test this, we unify arithmetic and structured dialogue into a single character representation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item We decompose inputs into \textbf{atomic characters} paired with 14 structural discourse tags (e.g., \texttt{<USER>}, \texttt{<THINK>}, \texttt{<CALL>}, \texttt{<RESULT>}, \texttt{<ANSWER>}).
    \item In arithmetic mode, characters follow decimal column coordinates $(p, \tau)$ (Table~\ref{tab:2d_matrix_example}A). In dialogue mode, characters are organized onto a \textbf{2D discourse matrix} (Table~\ref{tab:2d_matrix_example}B), where macro row $p \in \{0..4\}$ indexes the functional discourse turn and micro column $\tau$ tracks character position within that turn.
\end{enumerate}
We emphasize that discourse-mode coordinates ($p = \text{turn index}$) represent an architectural design choice to permit unified forward execution across arithmetic and tools---not a claim that 2D coordinates improve natural language text or tool routing over 1D. Indeed, our tokenizer ablations (Table~\ref{tab:tokenizer_dichotomy}) show that a standard 1D atomic-character model already scores 100\% (50/50) on structured tool calling. Evaluating tool agency serves to demonstrate that Decoupled 2D-RoPE does not compromise standard character-level capabilities, allowing a single compact model to perform both tasks without mode switching.

\subsection{Hypothesizing the 18M CBT Architecture}
\label{sec:18m_cbt_hypothesis}

To test this hypothesis and achieve maximal algorithmic and tool performance within a compact edge footprint ($< 20$M parameters, $\sim$36\,MB FP16 weights), we hypothesized the unified \textbf{Character-Based Transformer (CBT-18.95M)} architecture by combining four complementary mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Atomic Character Tokenization ($V=115$)}: Preserves exact character boundaries, eliminating subword tokenization chunking and enabling faithful 1-hop pointer copying for tool arguments.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled 2D-RoPE}: Rotates half of each attention head with macro row $p$ and the other half with micro column $\tau$, anchoring cross-attention invariant to sequence length in both arithmetic and discourse modes.
    \item \textbf{Coordinate Shift Masking ($k=32$)}: Randomly shifts macro coordinates during training, exposing rotary frequencies to an extended coordinate horizon ($p_{\max} = N_{\text{train}} + k = 15 + 32 = 47$) to enable zero-shot extrapolation up to 50 digits.
    \item \textbf{Weight-Tied Recurrent Depth ($R=3$)}: Unrolls $L=6$ physical Transformer blocks across $R=3$ recurrent passes ($L_{\text{eff}}=18$), adopted as an architectural choice to provide deep non-linear representation within a compact 36\,MB FP16 footprint without parameter inflation. As detailed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} and our 100k ablations (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}), recurrence is not claimed as an empirically necessary mechanism for addition (which extrapolates under 2D-RoPE alone at $R=1$) or tool routing.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope, Limitations, and Utility on Low-Resource Devices}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Why study in-model addition when a calculator tool exists?} Arithmetic here is a testbed for length generalization: it has a compact, exactly checkable algorithm and an unbounded length axis. We do not argue that an in-model adder is a practical substitute for a calculator; the tool-calling results are a separate experiment.

\textbf{Limitations}: (1) The coordinates $(p,\tau)$ are hand-designed and assigned by a rule-based parser, not learned; the length-generalization result is therefore conditional on correct coordinate assignment (Section~\ref{sec:coord_assignment}) and demonstrates column-aligned addition with a fixed-template scratchpad rather than general algorithmic induction. (2) Tool calling is bound to a small set of templates that overlap with training data, the OOD set contains only 18 prompts, near-exact copies are functionally failures, and we do not compare against a fine-tuned small off-the-shelf model. (3) Tool and 12d-ablation test sets are small (26 and 18 for tools, 14 for Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}; for arithmetic we supplement our 20 structured cases with a 315-problem random sweep on CBT-18.95M in Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d} and a 12,800-problem random sweep across four equalized 1--15d, $T=1024$ models in Table~\ref{tab:equalized_15d_1024}), every configuration is a single training run, and we report no seed variance. (4) In Table~\ref{tab:arch_comparison}, the 18.91M 1D baselines were trained on 1--12 digits with a 512-token context window; because scratchpad traces require $\approx 14.3$ characters per digit, traces for 25 digits ($\approx 360$ tokens) and 35 digits ($\approx 500$ tokens) fit within this 512-token window, where the baselines fail from positional drift rather than context clipping, whereas 50-digit traces ($\approx 715$ tokens) exceed 512 tokens (Table~\ref{tab:equalized_15d_1024} addresses this with equalized 1024-token models). At 70 digits ($\approx 1000$ tokens) and 80 digits ($\approx 1149$ tokens), traces approach or exceed the 1024 context window, confounding arithmetic failure with context truncation. (5) The only algorithmic task is addition; we do not test tasks where coordinates cannot be assigned by a simple parser (such as multiplication or sorting). (6) On a laptop CPU (Intel Core Ultra 5 125H, FP32), batched KV-cached inference runs at 141--394 tokens/sec (1.1--5.3 tokens/sec uncached single-stream), and we do not benchmark mobile ARM hardware or INT8 quantization. (7) Recurrent depth showed no benefit in our addition ablation (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}), and the 1.56M HRM-RoPE model (Table~\ref{tab:itemized_math}) also extrapolates to 20 digits using Decoupled 2D-RoPE alone, confirming that the 2D coordinate mapping---not recurrence or the multi-stage curriculum---is the primary driver of addition extrapolation.

\textbf{Practical Utility on Low-Resource Devices}: We make an explicit practical claim regarding scale. Scaling CBT as-is to frontier LLM sizes would not make practical sense: because CBT generates text \emph{character-by-character} and evaluates $R=3$ recurrent passes per character, its autoregressive inference latency is several times slower per word than subword BPE models. CBT's primary utility is in \textbf{low-resource local inference}: learning intent routing, structured JSON/API tool calling, and exact arithmetic within a tiny 100k to 18.95M parameter footprint suitable for on-device coprocessors. The 36\,MB figure reflects the FP16 weight size only: with $R=3$, per-token compute is roughly that of a $\sim$57M-parameter dense model and the KV cache grows with character sequence length. On a laptop CPU (Intel Core Ultra 5 125H, FP32), batched KV-cached decoding achieves 141--394 character tokens/sec across 16--100 digit traces (and 1.1--5.3 tokens/sec uncached single-stream); we have not benchmarked mobile ARM hardware or INT8 quantization.

\subsection{Unified Capabilities of CBT-18.95M: Sums, Multi-Tool Routing, and OOD Copying}\label{sec:v2}

Tables~\ref{tab:unified_18m}, \ref{tab:itemized_math}, \ref{tab:random_16_100d}, \ref{tab:stage3_all_tools}, and \ref{tab:ood_queries} present the comprehensive evaluation of our final \textbf{CBT-18.95M} model (\texttt{champion\_final.pt}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Extrapolation from 15 Seen Digits Across 16--100 Unseen Digits (Tables~\ref{tab:itemized_math} and \ref{tab:random_16_100d})}: Trained strictly on numbers of $1 \le N \le 15$ digits with shift masking $k=32$ (exposing macro column coordinates up to $p_{\max} = 15 + 32 = 47$), CBT-18.95M solves \textbf{20/20 (100.0\%; CI $[83.9, 100.0]\%$)} structured 16--50 digit sums (including $\underbrace{99\dots99}_{50} + 1 = 10^{50}$). To test beyond structured patterns and map the exact breakdown horizon, we evaluated 315 stratified random multi-carry problems across 13 lengths from 16 to 100 digits (Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d}; 70\% uniform random digits, 15\% dense carries with $a_i + b_i \ge 9$, 15\% cascade chains). Across all 6 tested lengths within the shift-masked coordinate support ($d \in \{16, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40\}$, $p \le 40 \le p_{\max}$), CBT-18.95M achieves \textbf{180/180 (100.0\%; 95\% Wilson CI $[97.9, 100.0]\%$)} exact match. Across the full 16--50 digit random suite ($n = 240$), it achieves \textbf{237/240 (98.8\%; CI $[96.4, 99.6]\%$)} final answer exact match and \textbf{238/240 (99.2\%; CI $[97.0, 99.8]\%$)} \texttt{<THINK>} scratchpad exact match, with \textbf{zero \texttt{(} delimiter errors} and zero off-by-one readout errors (the only 3 errors at $d=45, 50$ were single-digit substitutions at the $p \approx 47$ boundary). Beyond $p_{\max} = 47$, accuracy remains \textbf{90.0\% (18/20)} at 55 digits ($p \le 55$, $+8$ columns beyond shifted training support), then drops to \textbf{45.0\% (9/20)} at 60 digits, \textbf{6.7\% (1/15)} at 70 digits, and \textbf{0.0\%} at 80--100 digits. This empirically demonstrates that Decoupled 2D-RoPE generalizes reliably across unseen sequence lengths $N > N_{\text{train}}$ \emph{up to the maximum coordinate index $p_{\max} = N_{\text{train}} + k$ covered by shift masking during training}, and degrades once $p$ enters rotary angles never observed during training.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 All-Tools 1-Hop Alignment (Table~\ref{tab:stage3_all_tools}, 100.0\% Routing, 96.2\% Exact Args)}: In Stage 2 (\texttt{step\_7000}), applying 1-hop copy induction only to \texttt{search} caused prompts with varied prepositions (like \texttt{"What is the weather on Rome"} or \texttt{"How many miles is 25 km"}) to over-route to \texttt{search}. In Stage 3 (\texttt{champion\_final.pt}), transitioning \emph{all nine tool schemas} (\texttt{get\_weather}, \texttt{get\_time}, \texttt{calculator}, \texttt{unit\_convert}, \texttt{database\_query}, \texttt{fetch\_api}, \texttt{create\_event}, \texttt{file\_manager}, and \texttt{search}) to the entity-agnostic \texttt{<THINK>} + 1-hop \texttt{<CALL>} pointer architecture achieved \textbf{100.0\% (26/26) tool routing} and \textbf{96.2\% (25/26) exact argument extraction}, even routing unseen nonce cities (\texttt{"What is the weather in Bloumplik?"} $\to$ \texttt{get\_weather(city="Bloumplik")}). Caveat: Stage 3 was trained on the prepositions and templates used in this 26-case suite, whereas Stage 2 never saw them, so the Stage 2 versus Stage 3 comparison is not informative about generalization to new syntax. We did not measure template overlap between the 26 cases and the training generator, and we have no held-out paraphrase set.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization (Table~\ref{tab:ood_queries})}: On 18 unseen prompts, CBT-18.95M produced well-formed tags for all 18 (18/18) and routed 16/18 (88.9\%) to the intended tool. Among the 16 routed cases, \textbf{8 (50.0\%) copied the argument exactly}; 3 more were near-exact (\texttt{"existentialis"}, \texttt{"zagnarok"}, \texttt{"844 *19"}), giving 11/16 (68.8\%) as a secondary, more lenient figure. Because a near-exact string is an incorrect tool argument, exact match is the relevant metric. Exact copies include single words and short lowercase phrases (\texttt{"quantum physics"}, \texttt{"interstellar"}, \texttt{"hyperinflation"}, \texttt{"tiramisu"}, \texttt{"neutron stars"}, \texttt{"Reykjavik"}), but single-word copying is \emph{not} perfect (\texttt{"existentialism"}, \texttt{"Ragnarok"}, \texttt{"theremin"} fail). Multi-word capitalized proper nouns (\texttt{"Buenos Aires"} $\to$ \texttt{"Buris"}, \texttt{"Marcus Aurelius"} $\to$ \texttt{"Arcusnaus"}, \texttt{"Kuala Lumpur"} $\to$ \texttt{"Vuala"}) blend or truncate, plausibly because the synthetic nonce generator trains on single-word pseudowords (a hypothesis we did not test). An embedded digit (\texttt{"crispr cas9"}) and a preposition (\texttt{"Search for ..."}) each caused a mis-route.
    \item \textbf{Uncontaminated Unseen-Entity Evaluation of the v2 SFT Checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:unseen_ood})}: Because the Table~\ref{tab:ood_queries} entities and the 26-case tool suite overlap with training templates and entity lists, we ran a new test on 30 unseen entities (\texttt{benchmarks/evaluate\_unseen\_ood.py}; zero overlap with the training city, search-entity, and search-fact lists was verified) using a second SFT run, \texttt{champion\_v2\_final.pt} (v2; SHA256 \texttt{634bf18e...3a13}; 12,000 SFT steps, loss \texttt{0.0034}, wider shift range $k=45$, $p_{\max}=70$, expanded tool curriculum). These numbers are lower than the template-matched ones above, and we regard them as the more representative figures. The 56.7\% exact-copy interval spans 50\% and a one-sided binomial test against 50\% gives $p=0.29$, so we do not claim that v2 copies more than half of unseen arguments. On the 26-case suite, v2 fails \texttt{database\_query}, \texttt{fetch\_api}, \texttt{create\_event}, and \texttt{file\_manager} (one case each) and issues a spurious \texttt{get\_weather} call for ``Hello, how are you today?''; an embedded digit (\texttt{crispr cas9}) still mis-routes. The arithmetic sweep has only 10 problems per length (per-length CI lower bound 72.2\%) and stops at 55 digits, so the benefit of $p_{\max}=70$ is untested beyond that.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Isolated Component Ablations of CBT Mechanics (4.24M Token Budget)}

Because we tested multiple combinations of CBT components that achieved similar partial extrapolation scores before converging on our final configuration, we conducted a controlled 5-model factor ablation (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}) using the exact 100k-parameter budget (\texttt{100,672} parameters, $L=2, d_{\text{model}}=64, n_{\text{head}}=4, V=27$) and the exact same training token volume ($4,236,029$ tokens, 2,000 steps at batch size 32) as the 100k reference model:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{1D-RoPE Alone or With Recurrence Fails Completely (Models 1 \& 2)}: Under 1D-RoPE, neither $R=1$ (Model 1, 21.4\% overall) nor $R=3$ recurrence (Model 2, 14.3\% overall) solves even a single unseen 14--20 digit problem (0.0\% extrapolation; loss stalls above \texttt{0.13}).
    \item \textbf{Decoupled 2D-RoPE Is the Primary Factor (Model 1 vs.\ Model 3)}: The clean single-factor contrast for positional geometry is Model 1 (1D-RoPE, $R=1, k=0$) versus Model 3 (Decoupled 2D-RoPE, $R=1, k=0$): replacing 1D-RoPE with 2D-RoPE lowers training loss from \texttt{0.1864} to \texttt{0.0011} and raises accuracy to 100.0\% on seen 1--12d and unseen 14--16d sums (78.6\%, 11/14 overall).
    \item \textbf{Recurrence Is Not Needed for Addition in This Ablation (Model 3 vs.\ Model 4)}: Comparing Model 3 ($R=1$) against Model 4 ($R=3$) isolates recurrence: both score 78.6\% (11/14) and final loss is slightly worse with $R=3$ (\texttt{0.0017} vs.\ \texttt{0.0011}). We therefore make no claim that recurrent depth contributes to addition at 100k scale. We did not run $R=1$ versus $R=3$ at 18.95M, so whether recurrence matters at larger scale or on other tasks is untested.
    \item \textbf{Shift Masking and Readout Termination (Model 4 vs.\ Model 5)}: Comparing Model 4 ($k=0$) and Model 5 ($k=16$) isolates shift masking on this 14-case set; because they differ on only 1--2 test problems, the comparison is consistent with—but does not statistically establish—a benefit on internal scratchpad columns. At 12.8 epochs (5,000-sample pool), Models 3, 4, and 5 all score 78.6\% (11/14) on strict final string match. Inspecting the raw generation tokens of Model 5 ($2\text{D-RoPE} + R=3 + k=16$) on the 20-digit cascading carry $99\dots99 + 1$ reveals the following:
    \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
    &\texttt{<THINK>(0+9+1)=10(1+9+0)=10...(20 cols)...}\\
    &\texttt{(1+9+0)=10(1+0+0)=01</THINK>=1}\underbrace{\texttt{00...00}}_{19\text{ zeros}}\texttt{<EOS>}
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
    Inside \texttt{<THINK>...</THINK>}, Model 5's scratchpad was correct across all columns in the 14 test cases. The error is an \emph{off-by-one readout termination} after \texttt{</THINK>=}, where the model emits 19 zeros instead of 20 before \texttt{<EOS>}. Because the answer-digit coordinates are derived from the scratchpad column count via $p=\max(0, C-1-i)$ (Section~\ref{sec:coord_assignment}), the model itself must decide where the answer ends. Two plausible (untested) hypotheses for this off-by-one error are: (a) the clamp $p=\max(0, C-1-i)$ assigns $p=0$ to both the true units digit ($i=C-1$) and any extra overflow digit ($i \ge C$), making the last answer digit and the post-answer position share the same macro coordinate $p=0$; and (b) after shift masking moves $p>0$ by $\Delta \in [0, 16]$ while $p=0$ stays fixed, the $p=1 \to p=0$ boundary is under-trained at 12.8 epochs. When the exact same architecture is trained for 64 epochs on a 1,000-sample pool (\texttt{CBT-100k Ref}, 4.24M tokens) or scaled to \textbf{CBT-18.95M} (247.3M tokens), the readout error disappeared in our tests (14/14 for \texttt{CBT-100k Ref}; 240/240 random 16--50 digit problems with zero off-by-one errors for CBT-18.95M, Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d}), though repeating a 1,000-sample pool 64 times raises the possibility of overfitting to training length distributions rather than purely resolving boundary attention.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ablation Analysis: Coordinate Horizon Shifting, Decoupling, and Optimization}
\label{sec:reviewer_priority_ablations}

To rigorously isolate the mechanisms governing length generalization, we conducted three targeted empirical ablations (\texttt{experiments/run\_reviewer\_priority\_ablations.py}, Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_ablations}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Causal Shift Sweep ($k \in \{0, 16, 32, 64\}$)}: By varying the training position-shift offset $k$, we observe that the accuracy cliff moves strictly with $p_{\max} = N_{\text{train}} + k$. When $k=0$ ($p_{\max}=15$), accuracy drops precipitously immediately past the training frontier ($77.5\%$ at 16d, $17.0\%$ at 20d, $0.0\%$ at 25d). When $k=16$ ($p_{\max}=31$), accuracy exceeds $98.5\%$ through 30d before falling to $60.5\%$ at 35d and $0.0\%$ at 40d. When $k=64$ ($p_{\max}=79$), the model maintains substantial accuracy well beyond 40d ($69.0\%$ at 40d, $60.0\%$ at 45d, $41.0\%$ at 50d, and $20.0\%$ at 55d). This confirms that the bounded extrapolation horizon is not a failure of attention routing, but a direct consequence of the coordinate support envelope encountered during training.
    \item \textbf{Place-Only Ablation ($p$-Only, $\tau=0$)}: To test whether the intra-column micro-step coordinate $\tau$ is necessary or if place alignment alone suffices, we set $\tau=0$ during both training and inference ($k=32$). The $p$-only model achieves $99.8\%$ on seen 8--15 digits and averages $96.2\%$ across 16--30 digits ($100.0\%$ at 16d, $99.5\%$ at 20d, $99.0\%$ at 25d, $97.5\%$ at 30d, $85.0\%$ at 35d). This demonstrates that the macro place coordinate $p$ alone carries the underlying length-invariant algorithmic scaffold. However, full 2D-RoPE provides greater robustness near the cliff boundary ($58.0\%$ vs.\ $41.5\%$ at 40d; $30.5\%$ vs.\ $9.5\%$ at 45d), showing that $\tau$ provides secondary stabilization against phase dispersion during token generation.
    \item \textbf{1D Baseline Retraining with Extended Budget}: To determine whether 1D failures in Table~\ref{tab:equalized_15d_1024} were merely artifacts of insufficient training steps, we retrained 1D-RoPE with double the step budget (6,000 steps) and a tuned learning rate ($\mathrm{LR}=8\times 10^{-4}$). The retrained model attained only $28.0\%$ accuracy on 8-digit additions and collapsed to $0.0\%$ on 12- and 15-digit sums (as well as all unseen lengths). In contrast to 2D models which converge smoothly to zero loss, flat 1D coordinates in long-context scratchpads create an intractable optimization landscape for compact Transformers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Atomic Character and Structural Control Tokenizer ($V=115$)}

To eliminate BPE subword chunking distortions while retaining clear structural boundaries for multi-turn agentic dialogues, our tokenizer (\texttt{ScaledTokenizer}) defines a compact vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ of size $V=115$ for CBT-18.95M (the smaller CBT-200k and 100k variants use character subsets with $V=111$ and $V=27$ respectively), partitioned into two disjoint subsets:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{V}_{\text{ctrl}} \cup \mathcal{V}_{\text{char}}, \quad |\mathcal{V}_{\text{ctrl}}| = 14, \quad |\mathcal{V}_{\text{char}}| = 101
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Structural Control Tokens ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{ctrl}}$, IDs $0 \dots 13$)}: Consists of 4 standard sequence control tokens (\texttt{<PAD>}, \texttt{<BOS>}, \texttt{<EOS>}, \texttt{<UNK>}) and 10 paired discourse state delimiters that structure agentic reasoning:
    \begin{equation*}
    \small
    \begin{aligned}
    &\texttt{<USER>},\; \texttt{</USER>},\; \texttt{<THINK>},\; \texttt{</THINK>},\; \texttt{<CALL>},\\
    &\texttt{</CALL>},\; \texttt{<RESULT>},\; \texttt{</RESULT>},\; \texttt{<ANSWER>},\; \texttt{</ANSWER>}
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation*}
    \item \textbf{Atomic ASCII Characters ($\mathcal{V}_{\text{char}}$, IDs $14 \dots 114$)}: Contains all printable ASCII characters—lowercase letters (\texttt{a--z}), uppercase letters (\texttt{A--Z}), single digits (\texttt{0--9}), punctuation symbols (\texttt{+}, \texttt{=}, \texttt{(}, \texttt{)}, \texttt{\{}, \texttt{\}}, \texttt{"}, etc.), space, newline, and tab.
\end{enumerate}
During tokenization, a greedy left-to-right prefix scanner checks if the input substring at index $i$ matches any tag in $\mathcal{V}_{\text{ctrl}}$. If matched, the entire tag (e.g., \texttt{<THINK>}) is emitted as a single token ID; otherwise, the single character $s[i]$ is emitted as its atomic character ID.

\subsection{Decoupled 2D Rotary Position Embeddings (2D-RoPE)}

In \textbf{Decoupled 2D-RoPE}, every token $x_t$ is associated with a 2D coordinate pair $(p_t, \tau_t) \in \mathbb{N}^2$, where $p_t$ is the \textbf{Macro (Row) Coordinate} and $\tau_t$ is the \textbf{Micro (Column) Coordinate}. Within each attention head of dimension $d_k = 64$, we split the query and key vectors into two orthogonal 32-dimensional subspaces:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_{t}^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(p_t)\, \mathbf{q}_{t, :32}^{(h)} \\[4pt] \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(\tau_t)\, \mathbf{q}_{t, 32:}^{(h)} \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{t}^{(h)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(p_t)\, \mathbf{k}_{t, :32}^{(h)} \\[4pt] \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(\tau_t)\, \mathbf{k}_{t, 32:}^{(h)} \end{bmatrix}
\label{eq:2d_rope}
\end{equation}
For any coordinate scalar $c \in \{p_t, \tau_t\}$ and a 32-dimensional subspace vector $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$, $\mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(c)\mathbf{u}$ applies $2\times 2$ planar rotations across the 16 channel pairs $(2j, 2j+1)$ for $j \in \{0, 1, \dots, 15\}$ with $\theta_j = 10000^{-2j/32}$.

\begin{proposition}[Zero Phase Drift \& Scale-Invariant Column Alignment]
Let $q_t$ be a scratchpad query token in decimal column $p$ and $k_j$ be the corresponding operand digit in column $p$. Under Decoupled 2D-RoPE (Eq.~\ref{eq:2d_rope}), the pre-softmax attention logit $\langle \tilde{\mathbf{q}}_t^{(h)}, \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_j^{(h)} \rangle / \sqrt{d_k}$ is strictly independent of the total number of digits $N$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\left(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_t^{(h)}\right)^\top \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_j^{(h)} &= \left(\mathbf{q}_{t, :32}^{(h)}\right)^\top \mathbf{k}_{j, :32}^{(h)} \\
&\quad + \left(\mathbf{q}_{t, 32:}^{(h)}\right)^\top \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(\tau_t)\, \mathbf{k}_{j, 32:}^{(h)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because both the scratchpad token and the prompt operand digit belong to decimal column $10^p$, $p_t = p_j = p \implies \Delta p = 0 \implies \mathcal{R}_{\Theta}(0) = \mathbf{I}_{32}$, while $\tau_t \in \{0, \dots, 9\}$ is strictly bounded for all $N \in \mathbb{N}$.
\end{proof}
\noindent The proposition is conditional on the coordinates being assigned correctly: it states that a single logit between a scratchpad query token and its corresponding operand digit does not depend on $N$, not that the model computes carries correctly or that the coordinates are produced by the model. Moreover, Proposition~1 covers only scratchpad-to-operand attention; for the final answer digits after \texttt{</THINK>=}, the parser sets $p = \max(0, C - 1 - i)$ using the model's emitted scratchpad column count $C$, so answer-to-scratchpad alignment depends on the model having emitted the right number of scratchpad columns. Since RoPE depends only on relative offsets, the reason shift masking is considered during training is not absolute position but the large offset between scratchpad rows ($p>0$) and the prompt tokens at $p=0$ that appears at test length $N$ but was never seen at training length.

\subsection{Coordinate Assignment at Inference}
\label{sec:coord_assignment}
The coordinates are computed by a deterministic, hand-written rule-based parser (\texttt{compute\_2d\_coords} in the \texttt{scaled\_t4} model file) that is called inside the forward pass over the token ids; it is applied identically in training and in autoregressive generation, and the model does not predict $(p,\tau)$. The rules are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{Operands}: a digit in the first (second) operand gets $p$ equal to its distance from the \texttt{+} (\texttt{=}) token minus one, so units digits have $p=0$; $\tau=0$.
    \item \emph{Scratchpad}: inside \texttt{<THINK>}, each \texttt{(} token starts a new column ($p$ increments, $\tau$ resets to 0), and $\tau$ counts tokens since the \texttt{(}, capped at 9. The model therefore decides when to advance a column by emitting \texttt{(}; the parser does not count 10-character templates. Because column advancement is keyed on \texttt{(}, the model can advance $p$ only by emitting \texttt{(}, which also means a single spurious or missing \texttt{(} would shift all subsequent column coordinates. Across all 240 random 16--50 digit problems (Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d}), the 20 structured 16--50 digit problems, and the 14 ablation cases (Models 3--5 and \texttt{CBT-100k Ref}), \textbf{zero errors arose from a mis-emitted \texttt{(} inside \texttt{<THINK>}}; \texttt{(} column-count errors appeared only at $d \ge 60$ once macro coordinates exceeded the shift-masked training horizon $p_{\max} = 47$ (3/20 cases at 60d, 4/10 at 80d, 1/10 at 100d). The 1D baselines, by contrast, failed by repeating valid 10-character \texttt{(0+0+0)=00} blocks too many times rather than corrupting the \texttt{(} delimiter syntax.
    \item \emph{Answer}: after \texttt{</THINK>=}, the $i$-th digit emitted gets $p=\max(0, C-1-i)$ where $C$ is the number of scratchpad columns. The answer length is not given to the model; extra digits are clamped at $p=0$, so the unit digit ($i=C-1$) and any extra overflow digit ($i \ge C$) receive the same coordinate $p=0$. The model must therefore identify the termination point itself, and this $p=0$ clamp—alongside the $p=1 \to p=0$ shift-masking boundary—is a plausible cause of the off-by-one readout error in Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}.
    \item \emph{Discourse mode}: if \texttt{<USER>} is present, $p\in\{0,\dots,4\}$ is the index of the most recent segment tag (\texttt{<USER>}, \texttt{<THINK>}, \texttt{<CALL>}, \texttt{<RESULT>}, \texttt{<ANSWER>}) and $\tau$ counts tokens since that tag.
\end{itemize}
Thus the operand-to-column alignment (the operand and column ranks) is supplied by the parser, and the length-generalization result should be read as conditional on this hand-designed alignment. The parser also assumes a fixed prompt format (\texttt{A+B=}), and is a Python loop over the batch that is not optimized for speed.

During training, we apply \textbf{Selective Coordinate Shift Masking}:
\begin{equation}
p_t \leftarrow p_t + \Delta \cdot \mathbb{I}(p_t > 0), \quad \Delta \sim \mathcal{U}\{0, 1, \dots, k\}
\label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
with $k=32$ for CBT-18.95M and $k=16$ for the 100k ablation models of Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}. This exposes $p > 0$ arithmetic columns to indices up to $p = 47$ during 15-digit training (with $k=32$), while keeping the \texttt{<USER>} prompt ($p=0$) anchored at the origin. It is closely related to randomized positional encodings \cite{ruoss2023randomized}.

\subsection{Weight-Tied Recurrent Depth with \texorpdfstring{$1/\sqrt{R}$}{1/sqrt(R)} Scaling}

We unroll the $L=6$ physical Transformer blocks across $R=3$ recurrent passes per forward step ($L_{\text{eff}} = 18$ layers at zero parameter overhead) using a $1/\sqrt{R}$-scaled residual update:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{R}} \left(\text{Block}_l(\mathbf{x}, p, \tau) - \mathbf{x}\right)
\label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
The $1/\sqrt{R}$ factor is a heuristic residual damping choice. We do not derive a variance bound for it, and our ablation (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}) shows no benefit from recurrence for addition. We therefore drop the variance comparison between $1/\sqrt{R}$ scaling and unscaled accumulation that appeared in earlier drafts.

\subsection{Three-Stage Training Data \& 1-Hop Induction Curriculum}

Table~\ref{tab:training_data_spec} summarizes the three-stage training protocol that produced \texttt{champion\_final.pt} on dual NVIDIA T4 GPUs (FP16 AMP):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Joint Pretraining, 45,059 steps, 247.3M tokens, Loss \texttt{0.000094})}: Trained on a 50\%/50\% mixture of 1--15 digit column addition (40\% asymmetric lengths, 25\% symmetric lengths, 20\% cascading carries $99\dots9+b$, 15\% power-of-ten zero chains) and 6-category structured tool dialogues, with $-100$ loss masking on prompts and \texttt{<RESULT>} payloads.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Search Copy Induction SFT, 7,000 steps, Loss \texttt{0.004834})}: In early SFT tests, repeating an unfamiliar entity $X$ inside \texttt{<THINK>} before calling \texttt{<CALL>} caused a \emph{2-Hop Copy Bottleneck}, where character n-gram priors hallucinated phonotactic nonces (\texttt{"tomatoes"} $\to$ \texttt{"smoom"}, \texttt{"bloumplik"} $\to$ \texttt{"cheat"}). Decoupling \texttt{<THINK>} into an invariant intent statement and training on 65\% procedurally generated phonotactic pseudowords ($L_{\text{memorize}} \to \infty$) forced a \textbf{1-hop induction pointer head} ($p=2 \to p=0$) to copy unseen strings character-by-character.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 (All-Tools 1-Hop Annealing SFT, 4,000 steps, LR $5\times 10^{-5} \to 5\times 10^{-6}$, Loss \texttt{0.007532})}: Extended the 1-hop pointer copy formulation across all 9 tool schemas (\texttt{get\_weather}, \texttt{get\_time}, \texttt{calculator}, \texttt{unit\_convert}, \texttt{database\_query}, \texttt{fetch\_api}, \texttt{create\_event}, \texttt{file\_manager}, and \texttt{search}) alongside a 10\% 1--15 digit math replay buffer, achieving \textbf{100.0\% (26/26) tool routing}, \textbf{96.2\% (25/26) exact argument extraction}, and \textbf{100.0\% 1--50 digit sum extrapolation}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hyperparameters and Release}
\noindent Defaults in the training code (\texttt{train\_and\_eval.py} and \texttt{sft\_search.py} under \texttt{champion\_model/scaled\_t4/}): AdamW with weight decay 0.01, peak learning rate $10^{-3}$ with cosine decay, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, per-GPU batch size 32 on 2 GPUs, context length (block size) 1024 tokens with up to 4096 coordinate indices, $k=32$ shift range, $R=3$, FP16 autocast with gradient scaling. Stage 3 SFT uses a learning rate from $5\times10^{-5}$ to $5\times10^{-6}$. At $\approx 14.3$ characters per digit, a 50-digit trace requires $\approx 715$ tokens (fitting within CBT's 1024 context window, but exceeding the 512-token window of the 12-digit baselines). Traces for 25 digits ($\approx 360$ tokens) and 35 digits ($\approx 500$ tokens) fit within 512 tokens, where the 12-digit baselines fail from positional drift rather than context clipping. At 70 digits ($\approx 1000$ tokens) and 80 digits ($\approx 1149$ tokens), traces approach or exceed 1024 tokens, confounding arithmetic accuracy with context truncation. The code, data generators, and evaluation scripts are available at \url{https://github.com/NeithanT/CBT}, and model checkpoints are hosted at \url{https://huggingface.co/Neithanvarvas/CBT} (primary checkpoint \texttt{champion\_final.pt} SHA256: \texttt{79d908d3...ccaa}; v2 checkpoint \texttt{champion\_v2\_final.pt} SHA256: \texttt{634bf18e...3a13}; 18.91M 1D-Char \texttt{model\_1\_final.pt} SHA256: \texttt{06411d79...0c1f}; 1.56M HRM-RoPE \texttt{hrm\_rope\_1000\_examples.pt} SHA256: \texttt{18515686...f6ee}).

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Future Horizons}

\subsection{Positioning Within Prior Length-Generalization Work}
Prior studies have shown that standard Transformers fail to extrapolate arithmetic beyond training lengths under 1D positional encodings \cite{nogueira2021investigating,jelassi2023length,dziri2023faith,kazemnejad2023impact}. Several lines of work address this by modifying positional information or data formatting: Shen et al.~\cite{shen2023positional}, Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2024transformers}, and Duan \& Shi~\cite{duan2023interpolation} use explicit index hints and structured scratchpads; Ruoss et al.~\cite{ruoss2023randomized} randomize positional indices across a wider range during training (closely related to our shift masking in Eq.~\ref{eq:shift}); McLeish et al.~\cite{mcleish2024transformers} introduce Abacus embeddings that align digits of equal place value; and Fan et al.~\cite{fan2024looped} use looped Transformers for iterative algorithms. Our Decoupled 2D-RoPE is philosophically similar to Abacus embeddings and explicit index hints in that it supplies place-value alignment $(p, \tau)$ via a rule-based parser rather than learning it from raw text, while extending the coordinate grid to a two-dimensional $(p, \tau)$ RoPE subspace that accommodates both multi-character column scratchpads and multi-segment tool dialogues.

\subsection{Why CBT Suits Local Edge Inference Rather Than Brute-Force Scaling}
A central takeaway of our work is that \textbf{CBT is not meant to replace subword tokenization in massive trillion-parameter cloud LLMs as-is}. Because CBT generates text \emph{character-by-character} and executes $R=3$ recurrent passes per character, generating a 50-word paragraph requires $\sim 300$ sequential forward passes (compared to $\sim 65$ subword passes in a BPE model). At billion-parameter scale, this character-by-character serial bottleneck would make inference prohibitively slow.

However, at compact scales (\textbf{100k to 18.95M parameters}), individual forward passes involve a small matrix footprint (36\,MB of FP16 weights, though $R=3$ recurrence triples the per-token FLOPs to roughly that of a $\sim$57M-parameter dense model and character-level sequences expand the KV cache). On an Intel Core Ultra 5 125H laptop CPU (FP32), batched KV-cached decoding runs at \textbf{141--394 character tokens/sec} across 16--100 digit traces (and 1.1--5.3 tokens/sec uncached single-stream); we did not benchmark mobile ARM hardware or INT8 quantization, nor did we compare against fine-tuned small off-the-shelf subword models on tool calling. Subject to those practical constraints, CBT's ability to handle intent classification, 9-schema tool routing (26/26 on template-matched prompts; 8/16 exact argument copying on OOD prompts), and 16--50 digit sums (237/240 random; 20/20 structured) within a 36\,MB weight file suggests potential utility for:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Low-Resource Local Tool Routers}: On-device assistants on mobile phones, embedded IoT controllers, or local desktop daemons that parse user commands into structured API/JSON calls (\texttt{get\_weather}, \texttt{file\_manager}, \texttt{database\_query}) without cloud connectivity.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Symbolic Co-Processors}: Compact local modules that execute exact character-level formatting, arithmetic, and pointer extraction without subword hallucination.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Open Questions and Further Testing}
While CBT-18.95M shows that placing characters on a hand-assigned 2D coordinate grid $(p, \tau)$ yields 100.0\% accuracy (180/180) on random 16--40 digit sums, 98.8\% (237/240) across 16--50 digits, and 90.0\% (18/20) at 55 digits before degrading beyond the $p_{\max} = N_{\text{train}} + k = 47$ shift-masking horizon (Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d}), our OOD and ablation experiments leave several questions open:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to Epoch Reinforcement and Boundary Coordinates}: As shown in our 100k component ablations (Table~\ref{tab:component_ablations}), while several combinations of 2D-RoPE and recurrence achieved identical 78.6\% strict match at 12.8 epochs, the off-by-one trailing zero readout error at the $p=1 \to p=0$ boundary disappeared in our runs with higher epoch density (64 epochs on a 1,000-sample pool) or larger capacity (18.95M); however, these single-run comparisons do not isolate whether the fix came from boundary exposure, memorization of the small pool, or model scale.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Word OOD Pointer Spans and General Algorithms}: In zero-shot OOD testing (Table~\ref{tab:ood_queries}), two-word capitalized proper nouns (\texttt{"Buenos Aires"} $\to$ \texttt{"Buris"}) still experience partial compression, and our algorithmic generalization scope here focused on $\mathcal{O}(N)$ column sums rather than $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ multiplication or arbitrary graph algorithms.
    \item \textbf{Learned Coordinates and Other Tasks}: Emitting hundreds of scratchpad characters is an inefficient simulation of an ALU, the coordinates are hand-assigned, and extrapolation is bounded by the shift-masked coordinate support $p_{\max} = N_{\text{train}} + k$ (Table~\ref{tab:random_16_100d}). Important next steps are learning or inferring $(p,\tau)$, testing on a second task whose coordinates are not trivial (multiplication, sorting, string reversal), running multi-seed training, and comparing against equalized 1D baselines with randomized positions, index hints, and tuned Abacus embeddings \cite{ruoss2023randomized,zhou2024transformers,duan2023interpolation,fan2024looped}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Starting from the failure of a standard 18.91M-parameter Transformer and eight architectural baselines on unseen 18- to 20-digit sums (\textbf{0.0\% extrapolation at 18--20 digits}, despite 4.6--18.7\% on moderate 14--16 digit sums), our experiments suggest that position geometry is the dominant factor (character-level 1D-RoPE: 2.3\% across 14--20d; character-level 2D: 100\%), with subword tokenization secondary. We studied the \textbf{Character-Based Transformer (CBT)}, which generates atomic characters and structural control tags on a two-dimensional coordinate matrix $(p, \tau)$ via \textbf{Decoupled 2D-RoPE} and weight-tied recurrent depth ($R=3$, which our ablation found unnecessary for addition). Trained strictly on 1- to 15-digit sums with $k=32$ shift masking ($p_{\max}=47$) and multi-tool dialogues, \textbf{CBT-18.95M} (\texttt{champion\_final.pt}) solves 20/20 structured 16--50 digit sums, achieves \textbf{180/180 (100.0\%)} on random multi-carry sums across 16--40 digits and \textbf{237/240 (98.8\%)} across 16--50 digits (degrading beyond $p_{\max}=47$ from 90.0\% at 55d to 45.0\% at 60d and 0.0\% at 80--100d), routes 26/26 tool cases (25/26 exact arguments, on templates shared with training), and routes 16/18 OOD prompts, of which 8/16 (50.0\%) have exact arguments. Recurrent depth gave no measurable benefit for addition in our 100k ablation. Ultimately, our findings show that while 2D embeddings and character tokenization achieve high performance with exceptional \textbf{memory efficiency}, character-by-character recurrence is \textbf{not compute-efficient} at scale—meaning current subword LLMs remain well-suited for general cloud AI, whereas CBT is a candidate design for memory-constrained local tool-calling agents and a starting point for studying coordinate-aware architectures, pending the broader tests listed above.
